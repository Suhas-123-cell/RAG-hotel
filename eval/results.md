# Retrieval evaluation results

Generated by `python -m eval.retrieval_eval` on 2026-11-20 (commit `b24e691`).

- Queries: 63 hand-labelled (`eval/labels.jsonl`), 190 relevant documents in total
- Corpus: 140 documents, 306 chunks (semantic chunking), embedding model `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`
- Relevance is judged per source document; labels were written by reading the corpus, not by looking at retriever output
- Rankings are chunk lists mapped to source documents; a document is credited once, at its best-ranked chunk
- Recall@k = share of a query's relevant documents found in the top k chunks; Hit@k = at least one relevant document in the top k; MRR@10 and NDCG@10 use binary relevance

Short segments are merged into a neighbouring chunk instead. The same labels scored as follows on the old code (296 chunks, raw numbers in `results_before_chunk_fix.json`). ## Notes and caveats

- Small label set (see counts above); differences of a few points are within noise.
- List-style queries (for example `m02`, with 10 relevant documents) cannot reach Recall@5 = 1.0; Hit@5 and Recall@10 are the fairer numbers for them.
- The `RRF + support` row scores the context the pipeline actually hands to the LLM: 8 RRF chunks plus support chunks, support first, capped at 10. Support chunks come first, so rank-based metrics (Recall@5, Hit@5, MRR, NDCG) are penalised when a support chunk is irrelevant, and the cap can push RRF chunks out of the context. Recall@10 and Hit@10 do not depend on order and are the right columns for what the LLM sees.
- BM25 and dense scores are not comparable; RRF ignores them and uses ranks only.
- Answer quality is not measured here: that needs a live LLM and is not part of this script.
